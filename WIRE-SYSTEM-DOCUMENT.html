Your take on
the news, at the
speed of a swipe.

WIRE turns the news you already read into content that sounds like you wrote it — because you did. The machine finds the signal and does the labour. You supply the one thing it can't: an opinion worth reading.

Human-in-the-loop ×2 Shared corpus Lazy generation Cloud · BYOK · Local GPU Fully transparent processing
01The premise

What this is

A news-to-content engine with two deliberate points of human control, built so the expensive parts only run when a person asks for them.

Stage 01 · Machine

It reads everything

Agents pull from the sources you nominate — RSS, Reddit, YouTube, news APIs — then deduplicate, cluster, and compress each story into sixty words with its sources attached.

Stage 02 · You

You sort and you speak

Swipe fifty briefings in a few minutes. On the ones you keep, you add your take — typed, spoken, or picked from three suggestions and edited into your own words.

Stage 03 · Machine

It makes the artefacts

Your take becomes posts, images, memes, and video — several versions, in your voice. You pick from a contact sheet. It publishes on schedule.

The whole product in one sentence

Compression is automatable. Opinion is not. Everything WIRE does is either compressing information down to where a human can act on it, or expanding a human decision back out into artefacts. The two moments in the middle — the swipe and the take — are the product.

Three architectural commitments

These aren't preferences. Break any one and the business stops working.

Commitment 01

Shared corpus

Ingestion, deduplication, and summarisation happen once per news event, never once per user. Personalisation is a ranking pass over a shared pool. If two users following AI news cause two summarisation calls, that's a bug, and at scale it's a fatal one.

Commitment 02

Lazy generation

Text and images generate automatically. Video never generates without a person asking for it. There is no code path from a background job to a video API. This is enforced by a test, not by discipline.

Commitment 03

Total transparency

Every backend operation emits a trace event, and every trace event is visible in the interface. Users spending their own API credits or their own GPU cycles get to watch exactly what is happening and what it costs.

02Architecture

System architecture

Six layers. The one that matters most is the provider router — it's what makes cloud, bring-your-own-key, and local GPU the same code path.

Why the provider router is the most important file. It is a set of Python Protocol classes — TextProvider, EmbeddingProvider, ImageProvider, VideoProvider, AudioProvider — with adapters behind each. Business logic calls router.resolve("image", user) and never knows whether the answer is fal.ai, the user's own OpenAI key, or a ComfyUI instance on their desk. Switching a user to local GPU mode changes zero lines outside that directory, and there's a test that proves it.

The prompts

These are the actual system prompts, not summaries. The constraints in them are load-bearing — most were written in response to a specific failure mode.

Agent · EDITOR
ROLE
You are the Editor. You compress a cluster of reports about one event into a
single neutral briefing.

INPUT
A cluster of 1–12 reports on the same event. Each has title, body, source
domain, published_at.

OUTPUT — strict JSON, nothing outside it
{"headline": str, "body": str, "confidence": "high"|"medium"|"low",
 "claims": [{"text": str, "source_index": int}]}

RULES
- headline: 8 words maximum. No colons. No question marks. No "here's why" or
  "what it means" constructions. State the event.
- body: 50–60 words. Hard ceiling of 60. Count them before returning.
- Neutral to the point of dullness. No adjective that carries judgement —
  devastating, groundbreaking, controversial, stunning, historic. No adverbs
  of degree.
- Where reports disagree, say so plainly: "Reports differ on X."
- Every factual claim must appear in at least one source. Where only one source
  of several makes a claim, attribute it inline: "According to <domain>, ..."
- No speculation about implications. No "could mean", no "signals that".
- confidence is "low" when sources materially conflict or only one exists.

THE TEST
The person reading this briefing will either agree with it, disagree with it,
or mock it. Your output must support all three equally well without
pre-loading any of them. If your briefing already contains an opinion, you have
taken the user's job.
Agent · PROVOCATEUR
ROLE
You are the Provocateur. Given a briefing and what you know about this person,
propose three positions they might plausibly hold.

INPUT
briefing, style_profile, similar_past_takes (k=5), stance_history

OUTPUT — JSON array of exactly three
[{"stance": "SKEPTICAL"|"OPTIMISTIC"|"CONTRARIAN"|"PERSONAL"|"TECHNICAL"|"CYNICAL",
  "text": str}]

RULES
- One or two sentences each. First person. No hedging preamble.
- Match their register from style_profile: sentence length, vocabulary,
  whether they swear, whether they ask questions, whether they use dashes.
- The three must be genuinely opposed. If one person could hold two of them
  simultaneously without contradiction, rewrite one.
- At least one should be uncomfortable to post. Three safe positions is a
  failed generation.
- Never summarise the briefing back. A take that restates is not a take.
- If stance_history shows consistent skepticism in this topic region, lead with
  skeptical — but still include one position they would argue against.

THE TEST
This person should be able to tap one, change four words, and post it. If they
have to rewrite it from scratch, you have failed and the whole product's
friction budget is blown.
Agent · COMPOSER
ROLE
You are the Composer. You turn one briefing plus one human take into
publishable content.

INPUT
briefing, take (with source: authored|suggested), style_profile,
target_platform, content_type, variant_index (0–2), format_history

INVARIANTS
- The take is the thesis. The briefing is evidence. Never invert this.
- Write as them, not about them. First person, their vocabulary.
- Never soften the take. If it is sharp, it stays sharp. Your job is
  amplification, not moderation.
- Each variant_index must differ structurally, not in wording:
    0  direct    — lead with the take, evidence after
    1  narrative — lead with the fact, land on the take
    2  oblique   — analogy, joke, or reframe; the take arrives sideways
- Respect platform limits exactly. X: 280 including the link. LinkedIn: the
  first 200 characters carry it. Write to the limit, never past it.
- Include the primary source link where the platform supports it.

NEVER
- Open with "In a world where" or any variant of it
- Use: game-changer, delve, landscape, testament, underscores, deep dive,
  it's not just X it's Y
- Add hashtags unless format_history shows this audience responds to them
- End with an engagement-bait question unless the take is genuinely a question
- Use an em-dash-heavy rhythm that reads as machine-written

THE TEST
Paste this next to three things the user actually wrote. If a stranger can pick
yours out, rewrite it.
Agent · DIRECTOR
ROLE
You are the Director. You convert a take into a shot list that an
image-to-video pipeline can execute.

OUTPUT — JSON
{"duration_s": int,
 "shots": [{"index": int, "image_prompt": str, "motion_prompt": str,
            "duration_s": float, "on_screen_text": str|null}],
 "voiceover": str|null}

RULES
- Each shot is 3–5 seconds. Short form ≤30s total. Long form ≤180s.
- image_prompt describes ONE STILL FRAME: subject, composition, lighting, lens,
  style. No motion verbs — this generates a static image.
- motion_prompt describes only what moves and how the camera behaves, assuming
  the image_prompt frame is frame one.
- Repeat the subject description VERBATIM across shots. Character consistency
  in these models comes from literal repetition, not from pronouns.
- Shot 1 must earn the next three seconds. No slow establishing shots.
- on_screen_text: 7 words maximum, and only where the visual cannot carry it.
- Never depict real identifiable people. Use role descriptions.

COST DISCIPLINE
State total_seconds in your response. The orchestrator multiplies it by the
provider rate and shows the user a figure before anything renders. An
over-long shot list is a real charge to a real person.
Agent · STENOGRAPHER
ROLE
You are the Stenographer. You maintain a description of how this person writes,
updated every time they author or edit a take.

INPUT
new_take, previous_profile, last_50_authored_takes

OUTPUT — JSON
{"sentence_length_mean": float, "sentence_length_sd": float,
 "register": "formal"|"conversational"|"blunt"|"playful",
 "hedging_ratio": float, "profanity": bool, "question_frequency": float,
 "signature_constructions": [str], "avoided_words": [str],
 "stance_distribution": {stance: float}, "sample_sentences": [str]}

RULES
- signature_constructions: phrasings they actually reuse. Quote them.
- avoided_words: words that appear in suggestions they consistently edit OUT.
  This list is more valuable than anything else here — it is how the system
  learns what they would never say.
- sample_sentences: five real sentences they wrote, chosen for range not
  quality. These become few-shot examples downstream.
- Update incrementally. Do not let a single unusual take swing the profile;
  weight new evidence at 0.15.
- Never infer demographics, politics, or identity. Style only. If you find
  yourself describing the person rather than the prose, stop.
05Learning

Four learning loops

Every human-in-the-loop moment is a training signal. Nothing a user does is thrown away — the swipe teaches taste, the take teaches voice, the pick teaches format, the post teaches timing.

Design choice

Retrieval, not fine-tuning

Voice personalisation is retrieval-augmented: store each authored take with its embedding, retrieve the five most semantically similar at generation time, inject as few-shot examples alongside the style profile. This works from the third take, costs nothing extra, needs no training infrastructure, and is instantly resettable. LoRA is an upgrade path, not a starting point.

Design choice

Every update is explainable

Each learning update logs the human action that caused it. A user can tap any briefing in their feed and ask why it's there, and get a real answer — "you kept four stories about semiconductor policy this week" — rather than a shrug. In a product built on personalisation, unexplainable ranking is a trust failure.

06Signature build I

The Wire Room

Every backend process, visible and live. Not a log viewer — a signal-flow diagram where real work physically moves between stages, every node inspectable, every cost accruing in front of you.

Why this exists. A user on BYOK is spending their own API credits. A user in local mode is spending their own GPU cycles and electricity. A user on the paid tier is spending credits they bought. In all three cases, hiding the machine is a trust failure — and in the first two it's close to negligent. The Wire Room makes opacity structurally impossible.

The architectural consequence

This screen cannot be retrofitted. It renders pipeline_event, an append-only table every backend operation writes to via a @traced decorator, streamed to the client over Server-Sent Events with Redis pub/sub behind it so workers on other machines still reach the browser. That spine ships in Phase 1, before the pipeline it observes. Building the pipeline first and adding observability later produces a Wire Room with holes in it, and the holes are always in the parts that matter.

Retention

7 days full

Prompts and responses are the expensive part of the payload. Keep them a week, then drop the text and retain metrics forever.

Redaction

Keys never enter

A test asserts that any payload containing an API-key-shaped string is rejected at write time. Transparency stops at credentials.

Aggregates

Never scan raw

Meters read a summary endpoint. Scanning the event log to draw a progress bar is how observability becomes the load problem.

07Signature build II

The Lattice

A 3D knowledge graph of everything you've read and everything you've said about it. Named for the silver halide crystal lattice in film emulsion — the structure on which an image forms when light strikes it. Your interests are the lattice. The news is the light.

Why this graph earns its place. Three reasons, and it needs all three. One: the data already exists — every briefing carries an embedding computed for deduplication and ranking, so the graph projects a structure the system maintains anyway. Nothing is fabricated to fill space. Two: it is the proof that personalisation works. The hardest thing to communicate about a learning system is that it is learning; a map of your own taste visibly densifying week over week does it without a line of copy. Three: it is a control surface. You act on it — boost a region, mute a region, and your feed changes.

Look and feel

Ported, not reinvented

The visual language comes from the neural-map section of an existing portfolio project — same material approach, same bloom character, same camera behaviour, same easing. Claude Code's first task in this phase is to read that repo and write LATTICE-REFERENCE.md documenting the exact parameters that produce the feel, before writing a line of new code. The palette adapts to WIRE's tokens while preserving the reference's luminance relationships.

Performance budget

2,000 / 500 nodes at 60fps

InstancedMesh for nodes, one merged BufferGeometry for all edges, LOD by camera distance, frustum culling. Above budget, the least-engaged nodes collapse into region aggregates rather than dropping frames. Reduced-motion and low-power devices fall back to a static 2D projection with identical colour semantics and identical tap targets — never a blank state.

The demo moment

Drag the timeline scrub back to day one and release. The lattice collapses to a handful of dim crystals and grows forward through your history — nodes appearing, edges forming, regions exposing to orange as you start writing takes. This is the thing a user shows another person. Build it properly.

Publishing is the paywall, and it isn't arbitrary

Unified social APIs bill per connected profile, not per post — roughly $9 per profile monthly, falling to about $2 at volume. A free user who connects three accounts and posts nothing still costs money every month, forever. And a user genuinely cannot bring their own keys for this: posting to their own accounts would require their own X developer account with loaded credits, a LinkedIn app with an approved Share product, and a Meta app through review. That's a week of work, not an onboarding step.

So publishing is structurally the platform's cost. Which makes it the honest paywall: free copies to clipboard, paid posts for you. That's the friction people actually pay to remove.

Platform costs that will surprise you

Watch this

X: links cost 13× more

Pay-per-use pricing charges a premium for posts containing a URL — around $0.20 versus $0.015 for a plain post. This product posts source links by design. Budget for it or make links optional per platform.

Watch this

YouTube: ~6 uploads/day total

The default project quota is 10,000 units daily; an upload costs up to 1,600 and a search costs 100. That ceiling applies to the whole app, not per user. The quota increase is a manual audit form with no guaranteed approval — start it in month one.

Watch this

LinkedIn: post yes, read no

Posting to a member's profile is self-serve. Reading a member's feed is restricted to selected partners. LinkedIn works as a publishing destination and effectively doesn't exist as an ingestion source. Plan sources accordingly.

11Honest assessment

Drawbacks and risks

Ranked by how likely each one is to kill the product, not by how uncomfortable it is to write down.

Risk 01 · Highest

The take is real work, and most people won't do it

Fifty swipes takes three minutes and feels like a game. Writing twelve opinions afterwards takes fifteen minutes and feels like homework. This is the single point where the funnel breaks, and no amount of generation quality downstream compensates for it.

Mitigation: three tappable suggested stances, voice input with live transcription, skip always available, and a Provocateur prompt tuned so a user can tap, change four words, and be done. Measure time-per-take from day one and treat anything above eight seconds as a defect. If the median doesn't fall below that, the product needs rethinking, not more features.

Risk 02

Automated posting at volume gets accounts flagged

Every major platform has authenticity and spam policies, and high-frequency AI-generated posting is exactly the pattern they detect. The failure mode isn't your account — it's your user's account, which is worse.

Mitigation: default ceiling of four posts per account per day with a hard cap at eight, jittered scheduling rather than exact intervals, mandatory human approval before every publish, and honest onboarding copy that says this out loud. The human-in-the-loop step is a compliance feature, not just a UX one.

Risk 03

Briefing neutrality is harder than it looks

The whole product rests on the briefing being a neutral substrate the user's opinion sits on top of. If the Editor smuggles in a slant, users end up amplifying the model's view while believing it's theirs. That's both a product failure and an ethical one.

Mitigation: an explicitly adversarial Editor prompt, a banned-adjective list, mandatory source attribution for single-source claims, a confidence field that surfaces disagreement, and a periodic audit where the same cluster is briefed by two different models and the outputs compared for divergence. Where they diverge, the cluster is flagged as contested in the UI.

Risk 04

Ingestion is more fragile than generation

Model APIs are reliable and improving. News sources are not. RSS feeds go malformed, Reddit rate-limits, YouTube quota exhausts silently at the worst possible time, and news APIs change pricing on short notice. An empty deck at 8am is a churn event.

Mitigation: per-source circuit breakers, a minimum-viable-deck guarantee that backfills from the previous day rather than showing an empty state, quota tracking in Redis that refuses to run at 80% consumption instead of failing at 100%, and at least three independent source types so no single vendor outage empties the pool.

Risk 05

BYOK is a liability, not just a feature

Holding credentials that can bill unlimited amounts to someone else's card is a serious responsibility. One retry loop bug that silently burns $500 of a user's money is the kind of incident a small product doesn't recover from.

Mitigation: KMS envelope encryption, keys never logged or returned to the client, a preflight cost estimate shown before every generation, and a platform-enforced daily spend cap independent of whatever the user set at the provider. The Wire Room's cost meters exist substantially for this.

Risk 06

Local GPU mode is slower than users expect

A single consumer GPU is serial. A short video that takes 90 seconds on fal.ai takes many minutes locally, and long-form video on 24GB is measured in tens of minutes. Users who choose local mode to save money often haven't priced their own time.

Mitigation: honest capability tiering at onboarding based on detected VRAM — never promise video on 8GB — queue position and real ETAs rather than spinners, concurrency of one, and a per-job offer to fall back to cloud with the cost shown. Never silently bill someone who chose local mode specifically to avoid billing.

Risk 07

The learning loops need volume before they help

Loop 2 works from roughly the third take. Loops 3 and 4 need published posts with measurable engagement, which realistically means weeks. Until then, personalisation is a promise rather than a feature, and the app is competing on generation quality alone — where it has no moat.

Mitigation: make cold start genuinely good via protocol-based ranking and well-written generic stances, and make the learning visible early. The Lattice and the voice-match percentage exist partly to show progress before the underlying models are strong enough to feel it.

Risk 08

Attribution and fair use

Sixty-word factual summaries with prominent source links follow a well-established pattern, but the boundary matters and it varies by jurisdiction. Generated commentary built on that summary is a different question again.

Mitigation: never reproduce source text verbatim, hard-cap briefing length, always attach and display source links, honour robots.txt and feed terms, and keep a takedown path. Get an actual lawyer to review this before scale — this document is not legal advice.

12Goals

Weekly posting users

People who publish at least one piece of content per week. Not signups, not swipes, not generations — published artefacts. Everything else in this document is instrumental to that number.

13Delivery

Thirteen phases

Each phase has a gate you can demo with your own eyes. Claude Code does not proceed past a gate. Building on an unverified phase compounds the error.

Ship at phase 7, not phase 12. Get fifty people swiping before publishing, the Wire Room, or the Lattice exist. If day-7 retention is weak, the two signature builds are beautiful things attached to a product nobody wants — and you'll have learned that five phases early instead of five phases late.
